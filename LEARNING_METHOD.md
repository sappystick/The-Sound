# The Echo Ladder: a sound-first method for learning to understand a language from its own texts

**Status: a proposed method.** Its parts rest on established research. The combination and its speed have **not** been tested on learners. What is measured below is how fast *coverage of the text* grows, not how fast people learn.

Working engine for the Greek of John: `learn/` (app: `learn/index.html`, published as "Akouo").

## The idea
A text that reuses a small vocabulary intensively can teach itself, if its own sentences are met in the right order. John is such a text: 999 different words make up its 15,438 words, and the 100 commonest account for 77% of everything said.

The method orders the real clauses of the text into a **ladder**. Each step is one unaltered clause containing exactly one word the learner has not met. Every step is heard first, in the pronunciation of its time.

## The seven principles and their basis
| # | Principle | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sound first:** hear the clause before reading or explanation | Infants segment speech by rhythm and melody (prosodic bootstrapping); this project's finding that John was built for the ear |
| 2 | **Only real text:** every step is an unaltered clause | Nothing to unlearn; narrow reading of one author (Krashen) |
| 3 | **One new word per step** | Comprehensible input: understanding stays near complete while it grows |
| 4 | **Useful words first** | Zipf's law: a few words do most of the work |
| 5 | **Review inside new text:** known words return in later clauses at growing intervals; weak words are asked again sooner | Spacing effect (Ebbinghaus); expanding retrieval (Leitner) |
| 6 | **Grammar by colour:** each word coloured by its job (who acts, the action, what is acted on, of, to) | Dual coding (Paivio); Greek marks roles by endings, not order |
| 7 | **Recall, then confirm** | Testing effect: retrieval strengthens memory more than rereading |

What is new here is the combination. The course is generated by an algorithm from the text itself, so that reading, listening, vocabulary and review are one activity on authentic text.

## The algorithm (language-agnostic)
`learn/build_ladder.py`. Input: a text split into short units (clauses), each word with its dictionary form.
1. Start with nothing known.
2. Among unused units with the fewest unknown words (ideally exactly one), choose the one that scores highest on:
   - the frequency of its new word in the whole text (log);
   - plus 0.3 × how long ago its known words were last met (log);
   - minus 0.02 × its length.
3. Add its words to what is known. Repeat.

## What has been measured
| Measure | John (Greek) |
|---|---|
| Steps that add exactly one new word | 149 of the first 150 |
| New words per clause when reading John in its own order | 1.51 |
| After 25 steps | 26 words → 56% of all words in John recognised |
| After 50 steps | 51 words → 67% |
| After 100 steps | 101 words → 77%; 604 clauses fully readable |
| After 150 steps (recorded, about 10 minutes of audio) | 151 words → 83%; 864 clauses and 90 whole verses fully readable |
| After 400 steps | 401 words → 94%; 406 whole verses |
| Words learned early return inside later steps | median 3 more times within 150 steps |

**Control:** the same code on the Hebrew of Genesis gives 151 words → 70% of all words after 150 steps, with 149 of 150 steps adding exactly one new word.

## The engine
Per step: **Listen** (the clause in the Koine voice v2, words lighting as spoken) → **See** (meaning under each word, colour by role, the new word with how to say it) → **Check** (recall the new word). Before a new step, due words are asked again, played inside the clause where they were first met. Progress is stored in the learner's browser.

Sources:
- text: SBLGNT;
- meanings: Dodson's public-domain lexicon, with short overrides for the commonest words;
- grammar roles: MorphGNT tags;
- voice: `code/voice` (evidence rules only, slower pace, small room).

## Limits
- "Recognising a word" means knowing its dictionary form. Greek words change shape (εἶπεν is a form of λέγω). The app gives the meaning and a hint of the form, but learners still have to absorb the forms.
- Recognising 83% of the words is not understanding 83% of the meaning. Whole-clause readability is the stricter measure: 864 of 2,248 clauses after 150 steps.
- The voice is synthetic.
- No learner trial has been run.

## How to test it properly
Randomise learners with no Greek into three groups: this ladder; the same clauses in the Gospel's own order; a standard first-year textbook. Give equal time (for example, 10 sessions of 20 minutes). Then test:
- listening comprehension of unseen clauses from John;
- word recall after a week;
- reading speed.

Fix the analysis in advance.

## Using it for another language
1. A text the learner wants to read, split into clauses, with dictionary forms (a morphological tagger or lemmatiser).
2. A recording, or a voice with word timings.
3. A word list with meanings.

Run `build_ladder.py` on the units; record the steps; reuse the app template.
