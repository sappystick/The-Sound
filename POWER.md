# Words of power: an investigation

**The question.** Did ancient peoples hold a knowledge of sound, through voice, instruments, letters and names, that could heal, change consciousness, or act on matter? This investigation took that hypothesis seriously and let the ancient sources speak in their own terms. It then tested every claim against what the texts actually say and against what can be measured.

**How to read this file.**
- **Sources** are quoted from the primary texts in Greek or Hebrew; my translations.
- **Measured** results are computed by `power/investigate.py`; numbers are in `power/results.json`.
- **New readings** are interpretations offered as hypotheses, not established facts.
- **Physics** states what sound can and cannot do.

## 1. What the ancients themselves claimed

### The power is in the sound, not the meaning
| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Origen**, *Against Celsus* 1.25 (3rd c.) | "Those skilled in incantations report that the same incantation spoken in its own language achieves what it promises; but translated into any other tongue it is seen to become *atonon*, slack, and powerless. So it is not the meanings of things but **the qualities and properties of the sounds** that have a power." He applies this to the Hebrew names *Sabaoth* and *Adonai*, and to the name of Jesus, which "has visibly driven out countless demons from souls and bodies". |
| **Iamblichus**, *On the Mysteries* 7.4–5 (about 300) | Sacred "barbarous" (foreign) names are preferred to translated ones. Even if they could be translated, "they would no longer keep the same power". Such names have "much force, great brevity, less ambiguity". |

The word Origen uses for a translated spell, *atonon*, literally means "without tension, without tone": an acoustic word. In this theory the power of a word lives in its sound, in its **tone**.

### Vowels as hymn and instrument
**Demetrius**, *On Style* 71 (1st c. BC to 1st c. AD): "In Egypt the priests hymn the gods by means of the seven vowels, sounding them one after another; and instead of the flute and the lyre, the sound of these letters is heard for its euphony."

### Healing by music
**Iamblichus**, *Life of Pythagoras* 110–111: Pythagoras practised what he called "**healing through music**" (*hē dia tēs mousikēs iatreia*). In spring a lyre-player sat in the middle and a circle sang paeans. There were melodies made "against despondency and anguish", others "against anger and rage", others against desire. He preferred the lyre to the reed pipe.

### Healing by incantation: an eyewitness
**Josephus**, *Jewish Antiquities* 8.45–49 (AD 93/94):
- God gave Solomon the art against demons "for the benefit and healing of people". Solomon composed "incantations (*epōidai*) by which illnesses are soothed".
- "This healing is very strong among us to this day; for I saw a certain Eleazar … in the presence of Vespasian, his sons, the tribunes and a crowd of soldiers, freeing men seized by demons." He used a ring and a root at the nostrils, and spoke Solomon's name and incantations.
- **To prove it, he set a cup full of water or a footbasin nearby and commanded the demon, as it left, to overturn it.** This is the one claim in this material of a spoken word acting on a physical object.

### Creation by voice and letters
| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Sefer Yetzirah** 2.2–2.4 (Hebrew; date debated, 2nd–6th c.) | God carved the twenty-two letters, "hewed them, weighed them, exchanged them, combined them, and formed with them the soul of every formed being". They are "**carved with voice, hewn with breath, fixed in the mouth in five places**": throat, lips, palate, tongue, teeth. They are set "in a wheel with **231 gates**", every possible pair of letters (22 × 21 ÷ 2 = 231). |
| **Genesis 1** | "And God said … and it was so." |
| **John 1:1–3** | "In the beginning was the Word … all things came to be through him." |
| **Book of the Dead** (Renouf's translation, public domain) | "Words of power" (*heka*) appear 26 times. The god Thoth supplies them; the dead person declares "**I am the Master of the Words of Power**". The form "I am the …" occurs 148 times. |
| **Plato**, *Timaeus* 35b–36b | The world-soul is built from the numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 27 and filled in with musical intervals (9:8 tones). The cosmos is literally a musical scale. |

**New reading.** *Sefer Yetzirah* holds the earliest Hebrew phonetic classification of speech sounds by where they are made in the mouth, and it places that classification inside a creation story. Its authors held that the structure of speech is the structure of creation. Its 231 pairings are the same operation as the syllable matrix in this project's Sala design: a small set of sounds, combined in every way, to name all things.

## 2. What our data shows

### T1. Sacred sound is kept at the moments of power (measured)
In Mark, Jesus' own words are left in Aramaic, with a translation added, at exactly four moments:

| Moment | Mark | Matthew | Luke |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raising a dead girl | 5:41 ***Talitha koum*** | 9:25 no words given | 8:54 Greek only: "Child, arise" |
| Opening deaf ears, with a groan and spittle | 7:34 ***Ephphatha*** | no parallel | no parallel |
| Prayer in Gethsemane | 14:36 ***Abba*** | 26:39 Greek: "My Father" | 22:42 Greek: "Father" |
| Cry from the cross | 15:34 ***Eloi eloi lema sabachthani*** | 27:46 kept, in Hebrew form | 23:46 different words, in Greek |

His other Aramaic words in Mark are names and explanations (*Boanerges*, *Korban*). The two words of healing, a word of prayer, and the death cry are kept in their original sound, exactly as Origen and Iamblichus say a word of power must be. The later Gospels translate them away; Luke removes every one.

**New reading.** Mark preserves these sounds as words of power. Biblical scholars have noticed the resemblance to the "foreign words" of ancient healers. Our contribution is the count: four of four of Jesus' untranslated words fall at moments of healing, prayer or death, and the later Gospels progressively translate them out.

### T2. John's theology of the voice (measured)
"Voice" (*phōnē*) occurs 0.97 times per 1,000 words in John, against 0.38–0.72 in the other Gospels. In John the voice acts:
- **5:25, 28:** "the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live … all who are in the tombs will hear his voice".
- **11:43:** "he cried with a loud voice, 'Lazarus, come out'", and the dead man comes out.
- **12:28–29:** a voice from heaven; the crowd "said it had thundered". The voice is heard as a physical sound.
- **10:3–4:** the sheep know his voice.
- **18:5–6:** soldiers come to arrest him. He says "*Egō eimi*" (I am), and "they drew back and **fell to the ground**".

**New reading.** John 18:6 is the clearest scene in the New Testament where a spoken formula acts on bodies: two words, and armed men fall. The words are the same "I am" that the Book of the Dead uses as its word of power (finding 19 in this project). John's "I am" sayings were already measured as a structural ring in this Gospel (finding 2). In John's own narrative logic, "I am" is a word of power.

### T3. The seven vowels hold a hidden second melody (measured)
Sung on one held note, each vowel brightens a different overtone of that note, because the mouth's resonance picks out one harmonic. On 110 Hz, the seven vowels in Demetrius' order brighten harmonics **11, 19, 17, 21, 7, 17, 8**. That is a second, high melody floating above the held note, with no instrument. This is the principle of overtone singing (Tuvan *khoomei*, Tibetan chant).

**New reading.** Demetrius says the vowels were heard "instead of flute and lyre". Sounded in sequence on one note, they really do produce an instrumental melody of overtones. The claim may describe an actual acoustic technique, not a figure of speech. A recording to listen to is in `power/audio/seven_vowels.mp3`.

### T4. Recitation paces the breath into the healing range (measured, with a modern study)
Bernardi and colleagues (*BMJ*, 2001) found that reciting the rosary in Latin, or a yoga mantra, slowed breathing to about **6 breaths a minute**. At that rate heart-rate and blood-pressure rhythms synchronise and the baroreflex strengthens: a measurable, beneficial effect.

Assuming one breath per phrase at chant pace (2–3 syllables a second, with a 1.5-second breath):

| Text | Syllables per phrase | Breaths per minute |
|---|---|---|
| Ave Maria (Latin), half-prayer per breath | 31.5 | 3.5–5.0 |
| Lord's Prayer, Greek, per clause | 11.4 | 8.4–11.3 |
| Psalm 23, Hebrew, per half-verse | about 11 | 8.3–11.3 |
| John 1:1–5, Greek, per clause | 9.7 | 9.4–12.7 |
| Ordinary resting breath, for comparison | | 12–20 |

The phrasing of sacred texts, recited aloud, slows breathing below the resting rate and toward the 6-a-minute range where the measured cardiovascular effects appear. This is the most solid healing mechanism in the whole investigation, and every praying tradition has it in common.

### T5. Sacred formulas are built from continuous, voiced sound (measured)
Share of sounds that are continuous and voiced (vowels, m, n, l, r, w, y):
- Amen, Alleluia, IAO: 100%.
- Hosanna, Kyrie eleison, Maranatha, *Egō eimi*: 83–88%.
- All formulas together: **79%**, against 67% in ordinary narrative Greek.

They are made to be sustained and resonated, not spoken crisply.

*Amen* closes on a hum (m–n). Humming raises nitric oxide in the nose and sinuses about fifteen-fold over quiet breathing (Weitzberg & Lundberg, 2002). Nitric oxide widens blood vessels and has antimicrobial effects. Sample sizes here are small, so this is descriptive.

### T6. Plato's harmonic world, made audible
The *Timaeus* numbers span 4¾ octaves. Filled with 9:8 tones and 256:243 "leftovers", they give a 34-note Pythagorean scale. Its first octave on 110 Hz is 110, 124, 139, 147, 165, 186, 209, 220 Hz. A recording on a modelled lyre in a temple acoustic, ending on the 1:2:3:4 chord, is in `power/audio/timaeus_scale.mp3`.

## 3. What sound can and cannot do to matter (physics)

Sound is a pressure wave in air. The steady push it gives an object, its radiation pressure, is tiny at any level a voice can make:

| Source | Level | Weight it could hold up on 1 m² |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking voice at 1 m | 60 dB | 0.000003 g |
| Loud singing at 1 m | 100 dB | 0.003 g |
| Trumpet bell, close | 120 dB | 0.3 g |
| Close to a concert stack | 135 dB | 9 g |
| Jet engine at 30 m | 150 dB | 290 g |
| Ultrasonic levitator | about 160 dB | holds millimetre beads and droplets at standing-wave nodes |

- A singer radiates about 0.01 W of sound. In an hour that is 36 J: enough, in principle, to lift a 1 kg stone 3.7 m, if every bit of it could be captured, which no mechanism does.
- The mass-equivalent of that sound energy is 4 × 10⁻¹⁶ kg. Sound has no other link to gravity.

**What is real:**
- Resonance can shatter a wine glass with a sustained, exactly tuned note at about 100 dB or more.
- Sound arranges sand and water into standing-wave patterns (cymatics).
- Ultrasound levitates small objects in the laboratory.
- Focused ultrasound, millions of cycles a second at high power, is used in medicine to break kidney stones and destroy tumours.

None of these work at voice frequencies and voice power on human-sized objects.

**Verdict on matter and gravity:** no evidence and no mechanism. Josephus' overturned cup is the only physical claim in the material. It was a demonstration staged before an audience and cannot be checked.

## 4. Verdict, hypothesis by hypothesis
| Hypothesis | Status | Grounds |
|---|---|---|
| The ancients believed the power of a sacred word lay in its sound, not its meaning | **Established** | Origen, Iamblichus; Mark's untranslated healing words (T1) |
| Sound and recitation can aid healing | **Supported, for specific mechanisms** | Breath pacing toward 6 a minute (T4, Bernardi 2001); humming and nitric oxide (T5); music therapy reduces pain and anxiety in clinical trials. Not a cure for disease by sound alone. |
| Sound can change consciousness | **Plausible, modestly supported** | Slow breathing, sustained vowels and rhythmic repetition change heart rhythm and attention. Brain-imaging studies of chanting exist but are small. |
| Ancient chant used real acoustic techniques | **Supported** | The seven-vowel hymn produces an overtone melody (T3). Some ancient chambers resonate near 95–120 Hz, the range of a male chanting voice (acoustic surveys of Newgrange and the Hal Saflieni Hypogeum); claims of special mental effects there rest on very small studies. |
| Sound can lift, move or transform heavy matter, or affect gravity | **Not supported** | Section 3: forces from voices are thousands to millions of times too small, and there is no coupling to gravity |
| Creation by word is a shared ancient idea | **Established as belief** | Genesis, John, *Sefer Yetzirah*, Memphite Theology (Ptah creates by heart and tongue), Book of the Dead |

## 5. Experiments the team could run
1. **Breath and heart.** Recite the Lord's Prayer in Greek, Psalm 23 in Hebrew and the Ave Maria in Latin at chant pace while wearing a heart-rate-variability monitor. Compare silent reading and ordinary speech. Prediction from T4: slower breathing and higher heart-rate variability during recitation.
2. **The hum.** Measure exhaled nasal nitric oxide during sustained "Amen", the seven vowels, and quiet breathing (handheld meters exist).
3. **The seven vowels.** Record trained singers chanting α ε η ι ο υ ω on one note. Check by spectrum whether listeners hear the overtone melody, and compare it with the prediction in T3.
4. **Rooms.** Measure the resonant frequencies of a surviving synagogue or chapel, and test whether chanting at the room's resonance changes listeners' reports, with the listeners not told the frequency.
5. **Translation test (Origen's claim).** Have listeners rate the same formula in its original sound and in translation for felt force, without telling them which is which.
