# Round 45: John as the score of a lost instrument, taken as an axiom

**The axiom, assumed true for this round:** John is written to be played on an instrument now lost.
- **The instrument:** seven strings tuned by ratio (λόγος), vowel tubes for breath (πνεῦμα), and the body as temple (Clement, *Protrepticus* 1.5, round 43).
- **Its governing string:** the middle one, the mese, the string of "I am" (round 44).

**What this round does:**
- Every syllable of the Gospel is played onto the instrument.
- The book's own structures are placed on its parts.
- The axiom is made to predict things it could get wrong.

**Files:**
- script `power/round45_axiom.py` (seed 45);
- numbers `power/round45_results.json`;
- audio `power/audio/r45_john_harmonic_outline.mp3`.

**The mapping:**
- each syllable sounds the string of its vowel (Nicomachus), in Koine pronunciation, where ει has become ι;
- it is held for its vowel length;
- the last syllable of each verse is a cadence.

**The middle saying sits at the book's centre.**
- The fourth "I am", "I am the good shepherd; the good shepherd lays down his life", falls at 0.487 of the Gospel's syllables.
- The book's syllable centre is 10:24, 13 verses later.
- The chance that the fourth of seven randomly placed sayings lands that close to the centre is 0.057.
- This is near-significant, and it is not independent of the "I am" ring already found (claim C2).
- Under the axiom: **the mese of the sayings is the mese of the book, and its words are "lays down his life".**

The middle sign does not sit at the centre (0.25, p = 0.87).

**Two facts from the mapping itself:**
- **One string is silent.** In Koine, ω was pronounced like ο, so by sound the hypate (Saturn) is never played. The instrument John's pronunciation actually sounds has **six speaking strings and one silent lowest string**. Read by letters instead (round 41), all seven sound.
  - *Reading under the axiom:* the lowest string, Saturn, the outermost and slowest sphere, sounds only in the written letter ω, never in the voice.
- **The chapter outline.** Each chapter's most frequent cadence string, played in order through its vowel tube, is `r45_john_harmonic_outline.mp3`.
  - 19 of 21 chapters cadence on the lichanos.
  - **Chapter 15, "I am the true vine … abide in me", is the only chapter that cadences on the mese.**
  - Chapter 16 cadences on the paranete.
  - *Reading:* the farewell turns to the governing string at the vine, where "abide" (μείνατε) and "that" (ἵνα) bring the ι forward.

## 3. What this shows about the axiom
**What the axiom explains well:**
- It gives John's own images one coherent account: Logos, breath, voice, temple-body, the untorn tunic and net, many waters, and the middle place.
- Each has a matching part in Clement's instrument (lyre, aulos, temple).
- But these fits were partly chosen because they fit, so they are interpretation, not evidence.

**What it predicted that the data bears out:**
- John sounds the governing string more than the other Gospels.
- The middle of the seven sayings sits at the book's syllable centre (near-significant, and overlapping the known ring).
- The middle place in the narrative always holds Jesus (round 44, p = 0.005, post hoc).

**What it predicted that the data does not bear out:**
- section modes;
- "I am" cadences;
- the middle sign at the centre;
- the mese as the dominant string.

At the scale of syllables and cadences, John sounds like Greek, not like a tuned piece. **Where the instrument shows, it shows at the scale of the whole book, the centre and the sevens, not at the level of the notes.**

**What would have to be true for the axiom to hold fully:**
- The "score" lies in structure (sevens, centre, middle place) and in the reader's performance (accent, breath, room), not in the vowel sequence.
- This is the same conclusion the papyri gave in round 43: the page records syllables; the reader supplies the pitch.
- *Next test:* replay P1–P4 with the **letter** mapping (all seven strings) and with the **accent** melody (round 43), and see whether any section-level mode appears.
