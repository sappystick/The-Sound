**What the counts show:**
- **"In the midst" is above all a Hebrew-Bible motif** (12–13 per 10,000).
- **Twelve is the New Testament's** (the twelve disciples, tribes, gates, foundations).
- **The cube and the square belong to the philosophers** (Plato, Philo, the Orphica).

**Where all three meet in one chapter:**

| Chapter | What is there |
|---|---|
| **Exod 28 and 39** | the breastpiece: square, twelve stones, worn by the high priest who goes into the middle |
| **1 Kgs 7** | the bronze sea on twelve oxen, square panels, in the court |
| **Ezek 43** | the altar hearth 12 × 12, "square on its four sides"; "**the place of my throne … where I will dwell in the midst of the children of Israel for ever**" (43:7) |

Revelation 21 (twelve gates, a square or cube city) uses "middle" a verse later (22:2), so it just misses the count.

**Reading:** the three motifs fuse in exactly two places: **the high priest's breastpiece** (the twelve worn over the heart, carried into the centre) and **Ezekiel's altar at the dwelling-in-the-midst**.

## 3. If the pyramid was this matrix, what was it for, and how could the Bible encode that?
**What Egyptology says the pyramid was for (K):**
- the king's tomb and the engine of his transformation;
- it protects the body, turns the king into an *akh* (a luminous spirit), and sends him up to join the stars and Ra;
- it sits at the centre of the world as the primeval mound, from which creation rose;
- it is a cosmos in miniature, organised around one person's ascent.

**How the Bible carries the same matrix with the purpose turned around (Reading, every quotation checked):**
1. **The direction is reversed.** In Egypt the king goes up to the gods. In Exodus God comes down: "Let them make me a sanctuary, **that I may dwell in their midst**" (Exod 25:8). John: "the Word became flesh and **tabernacled** among us" (1:14). Revelation: "the dwelling of God is with men" (21:3).
2. **The occupant of the centre is changed.** The pyramid's centre holds a dead king. The sanctuary's centre must not: "the place of my throne … where I will dwell in the midst … they shall no more defile it … by the corpses of their kings" (Ezek 43:7). The ark holds the covenant tablets, not a body.
3. **Who stands around the centre.** In Egypt, one man ascends. In Israel, **twelve tribes encamp around the middle**, three on each side (Num 2), and the high priest wears the same twelve over his heart into the middle (Exod 28). The New Jerusalem is a cube with twelve gates (Rev 21).
4. **The ladder is reassigned.** The Pyramid Texts' stairway for the king becomes Jacob's ladder, with angels going up and down (Gen 28:12). In John it becomes a person: "you will see heaven opened, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of Man" (1:51).
5. **The stone.** "The stone the builders rejected has become the head of the corner" (Ps 118:22). "Who laid its cornerstone?" (Job 38:6). Both are applied to Christ (Mark 12:10; Acts 4:11; 1 Pet 2:7). *Rosh pinnah* is a corner or foundation stone, not an apex; the popular "capstone of the pyramid" reading is a modern Reading.
6. **Leaving the pyramid world.**
   - Josephus says the Egyptians "wore out our race **building pyramids**" (*Ant.* 2.203). Historically the pyramids are far older, but this is how first-century Jews told it.
   - In Exodus, the people leave Egypt, and almost the next thing commanded is a portable tent of wood and cloth with God in the middle (Exod 25).
   - **Reading:** the tabernacle as the answer to the pyramid. Movable, not monumental. God descending, not a king ascending. The people around the centre, not one body inside it.
7. **Isaiah 19:19–20.** "In that day there will be **an altar to the LORD in the midst of the land of Egypt, and a pillar at its border** to the LORD, and it will be **for a sign and a witness**."
   - Pyramid theorists (Piazzi Smyth; C. T. Russell) read this as the Great Pyramid. The text is about a future altar and standing stone, not a pyramid.
   - **But it was acted on in antiquity.** Josephus records that Onias IV built a Jewish temple in Egypt, "trusting above all in the prophet Isaiah", at Leontopolis **in the nome of Heliopolis** (*Ant.* 13.62–64). Heliopolis was the centre of the sun cult and of the *benben* stone that the pyramid's shape derives from.
   - **C:** the one real Jewish temple in Egypt stood beside the home of the pyramid's sacred stone, built on the strength of Isaiah's "altar in the midst of Egypt".

## Conclusion
**What is supported:**
- The cube, the twelve and the centre are real, measurable motifs.
- They meet in the breastpiece and in Ezekiel's dwelling-in-the-midst.
- Israel's sanctuary shares a well-documented architectural grammar with Egypt: the 2:1 royal camp, the shrine chest with winged guardians, the dark inner room, the primeval centre, and a common cubit.

**The strongest reading of "the purpose encoded":**
- The Bible keeps the matrix (centre, cube, twelve, inner room, ascent) and **reverses its purpose**.
- **Egypt:** a dead king ascends from a stone mountain.
- **Bible:** the living God descends into a tent in the middle of twelve tribes, the dead are excluded from the centre, and the ladder becomes a person.

**Not shown:** that the pyramid was built from these instructions, or that the Bible hides its blueprint. The relationship is shared architectural language and deliberate theological answer, not a hidden encoding.
