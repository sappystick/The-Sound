## 2. The theory: the tabernacle and temple as designs for a lost instrument (exploratory)
### E1. Their dimensions are in consonant ratios
For the 18 sacred structures and objects whose dimensions the text gives:
- **71%** of the ratios between their dimensions are consonant (1:1, 2:1, 3:2, 4:3, 5:4, 5:3, 6:5).
- Random pairs of the same numbers give **28%**.

| Object (text) | Its ratios | In musical terms |
|---|---|---|
| **Table of showbread** (Exod 25:23) | 2 : 1 : 1½ | **octave, fifth and fourth: the three Pythagorean consonances in one object** |
| **Ark** (25:10) | 2½ : 1½ : 1½ | 5:3 (major sixth), 1:1 |
| Altar of burnt offering (27:1) | 5 : 5 : 3 | 1:1, 5:3 |
| Incense altar (30:2) | 1 : 1 : 2 | 1:1, octave |
| **Holy of Holies** (1 Kgs 6:20) | 20 : 20 : 20 | unison ×3 |
| **Solomon's house** (6:2) | 60 : 20 : 30 = 6 : 2 : 3 | octave, fifth, twelfth |
| Bronze sea (7:23) | 10 : 5 : 30 | octave, twelfth, 6:1 |
| Bronze stand (7:27) | 4 : 4 : 3 | 1:1, fourth |

**But the secular measured objects** (Og's bed, the House of the Forest, the Hall of Pillars, Nebuchadnezzar's statue, Zechariah's scroll) reach **57%** (4 of 7). The difference is not significant (p 0.36).
- **Simple ratios are how ancient builders designed generally.** The sacred objects are not unique in this.
- What is distinctive is a few objects that contain *exactly* the canon's consonances: the table (octave, fifth, fourth) and the house (6 : 3 : 2).

### E3. What simple ratios do to a room: the room becomes one harmonic series
**The rule:** a room's lowest resonance along each axis is c / 2L. When the axes are in small whole-number ratios, these resonances are harmonics of one fundamental, and higher modes pile up on the same notes.

| Room (common cubit 0.445 m) | The three axes as harmonics | Fundamental |
|---|---|---|
| **Solomon's house** 60 × 20 × 30 | **1, 3, 2** | 6.4 Hz |
| **Tabernacle** 30 × 10 × 10 | **1, 3, 3** | 12.9 Hz |
| **Holy of Holies** 20 × 20 × 20 | **1, 1, 1** | 19.3 Hz |

**Calc:**
- These rooms are tuned like a string: their axial resonances are the first harmonics of a single note.
- Above that note they keep stacking. Round 38 measured 73 modes on 15 frequencies in a cube, against 56 in an ordinary room.
- **Caveat:** any room with whole-number dimensions is harmonic of *some* very low note. What these rooms have is *small* numbers, which make the fundamental high and the coincidences many.

**Reading under the theory:**
- If the Holy of Holies, the tabernacle and Solomon's house were designed as resonators, their proportions are the right ones: the room reinforces whole harmonic series.
- In that reading, the room is the soundbox of the instrument. The Levites' voices, the trumpets and the lyres are its strings and reeds.
- Clement's "temple" (round 43) is that soundbox.

### E2. Are there "played" passages in an undeciphered language?
If parts of scripture were played or sounded rather than spoken, they should look like non-language: words repeated far beyond normal use. A repetition detector was run over the Hebrew Bible (18,766 verses) and the Greek New Testament.

- **Positive control: Isaiah 28:10,** *ṣaw lāṣāw ṣaw lāṣāw qaw lāqāw qaw lāqāw*, ranks **12th of 18,766**. Its words are probably the letter names *ṣade* and *qof*, or a sound mimicry. It is the classic candidate for a non-lexical sound passage (K).
- **The most repetitive verse in the Hebrew Bible is the menorah's construction**, Exod 37:21 (and 25:35), "a knob under two branches … a knob under two branches …". The seven-branched lamp, read by Philo as the choir of the planets (round 50), is built in the text's most rhythmic verse.
- **Other top Hebrew verses** are lists and formulas: the boundaries of Ezek 48:16, Jeremiah's kings, the pillars Jachin and Boaz (1 Kgs 7:21), Ps 94:3 ("how long … how long").
- **Top Greek verses:** Revelation's gates and tribes (21:13; 7:6–8); Matthew's genealogy. And **Rev 18:22**: "the **sound of harpers and musicians, of flute-players and trumpeters, shall be heard no more**".
- **John's top verses:** 3:31 ("he who comes from above is above all … he who is of the earth …"), 13:32 and 1:1. This is rhetorical repetition in normal Greek.

**Result:**
- **N** for an undeciphered "played" layer in John. The detector finds no non-lexical passage there.
- **O** for the Hebrew Bible: Isaiah 28:10 and the menorah verses are the candidates. One is a sound-play of letter names; the other is the most rhythmic description of an object in scripture.
- The era's real undeciphered sound language sits *outside* scripture: the magical vowel-strings and names of the papyri (round 38).
- Inside the Gospels, the untranslated sound words are Mark's Aramaic (*talitha koum*, *ephphatha*).

## 3. What this round shows
1. **John is not a timed score.** No string-ratio timing, and no cues at the joints. Its structure is in the arrangement and the tempo, not in the clock.
2. **The sacred buildings' proportions are those of a tuned resonator.**
   - Solomon's house, the tabernacle and the Holy of Holies resonate as single harmonic series.
   - The table holds the octave, fifth and fourth.
   - Designed objects of the period generally use simple ratios, so this is consistent with the instrument theory but not proof of it.
3. **There is no hidden played language in John.** The candidates for sound-only passages are in the Hebrew Bible (Isa 28:10; the menorah's construction) and in the magical papyri, not in the Gospel.
