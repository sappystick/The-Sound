Was the text written as performance?

The idea: the written Greek records movement and action as well as speech, a kind of three-dimensional score. First test: turn every verb of motion into a step on three axes (up/down, in/out, toward/away) and count body actions (stand, sit, bow, weep, breathe on, wash), then compare John with Matthew, Mark and Luke.

Vertical movement (ascend / descend) per 1,000 words

All motion together is level (John 19.7; Matthew 20.2; Mark 19.8; Luke 16.8). Only the vertical axis stands out: John's heaven–earth movement.

Speech and action

John switches between speech and action less often than chance (0.32 against 0.44 for shuffled order): he writes in long blocks of discourse and long blocks of action. In 19% of his verses, speech and action happen together (Matthew 17%, Mark 16%, Luke 15%).

The trial before Pilate: in and out

The inside/outside staging scholars describe is visible, but with only 7 moves it is not more regular than chance (p = 0.46).

Verdict so far

The text does carry staging (entrances, exits, postures, gestures), and John's vertical axis is distinctive, but there is no evidence yet of a hidden spatial notation beyond what any narrative carries. How to test it properly:

  1. Compare John's staging with a real performance script in Jewish Greek: Ezekiel the Tragedian's Exagoge (2nd century BC), and with Greek tragedy, where stage action is written into the speech.
  2. Score deixis (this, here, behold, now) and second-person address against the movement track: in a performance script these cluster where actors move.
  3. Give actors only the Greek and ask them to stage a scene; compare their blocking with the movement track.
  4. Use the sound model's timing to test whether actions fall on breath boundaries, as stage business does.

What it means

For reading John

John is a layered text that says so itself, written for the ear in Greek by a Semitic voice. His symbols point to particular passages: Isaiah's Servant, Ezekiel's river, Leviticus' purification, Jacob's ladder, Zechariah's Tabernacles. His teaching about the self is in his grammar: seven images without titles, "not from myself", the last "I" spent bearing witness to the truth.

What can change

The order of the books cannot: rings, hidden orders and number codes all tested negative. The reading can: present John in breath groups and theme blocks with centres marked, keep Greek word-chains intact in translation (one word for "divided / not torn"; both senses of ἄνωθεν), read him beside the passages he points to, and treat sound-alike variants as one heard text.
