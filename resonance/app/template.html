    How a session is made

    Beat. Binaural mode sends a slightly different tone to each ear, and the brainstem hears the difference as a beat. Monaural mode puts the beat into the sound itself, and isochronic mode pulses one tone. The carrier is a degree of the drone's own scale, moved by octaves into the device's best band, so the beat tone belongs to the chord.

    Breath. The drone swells as you breathe in and falls as you breathe out. A faint air sound marks each in-breath. The rate can glide from start to end; about six breaths a minute is the rate with the strongest evidence.

    Voice. On every out-breath, a voice sings one vowel at the root pitch and closes on a hum. The vowels come from the chosen language: Sumerian, Akkadian, Middle Egyptian, Coptic, Tiberian Hebrew, Galilean Aramaic, Koine or Attic Greek, or the seven vowels of the magical papyri. The PGM XIII orders follow the seven directions of the vowel square.

    Root and room. The root can come from a described building: the strongest resonance of Solomon's 20-cubit cube (123.4 Hz, where 15 room modes coincide), the Tabernacle, the temple hall, the King's Chamber, or the 111 Hz reported for the Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum. Reverberation is synthesised from that room's computed modes.

    Device rules. Bone conduction reaches both inner ears almost equally, so binaural is switched to monaural and nothing is played below 250 Hz. Speakers mix the two channels in the air, so binaural becomes isochronic.

    Verification. verify.py renders each configuration in a list and measures the result. Subtracting the left channel from the right cancels every shared layer and leaves only the two beat tones, whose spacing is read off the spectrum. Monaural and isochronic beats are read from the envelope, and breath from the slow loudness swell. All 10 presets pass. On its first run the check caught a real bug: the temple-hall root had fallen to 9.6 Hz.

    What is and is not claimed. A 40 Hz beat reliably drives the auditory cortex, and slow breathing reliably raises heart-rate variability. That slower beats entrain other brain rhythms, or change mood, is a mixed literature. The ancient vowel and room values are reconstructions or computations from the texts. Any wider effect is a hypothesis for the experiment protocols (E1, E2, E5) to test with listeners. This page plays audio; it does not measure you.

    In this page, isochronic pulses are raised-sine shaped and the voice uses three band filters. The Python engine uses a glottal pulse through a formant cascade. The settings are shared, and the sound is close but not sample-identical.
